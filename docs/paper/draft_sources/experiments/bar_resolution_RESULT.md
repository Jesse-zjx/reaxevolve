## Which clause carried the weight

3 of the rejected candidates had **exactly one imaginary mode**. They are genuine first-order saddles, just not the ones that connect the stated reactant and product — the imaginary frequencies are far from the deposited -2042.5 cm⁻¹, and the IRC runs to different structures.

A bar that asked only "is this a proper transition state?" would have accepted them. The clause that rejected them is the endpoint criterion: the IRC has to relax to the stated neutral reactant and product. That is the clause doing the work, and this control is the evidence for it.

## Limits of this control

- One step, one guess method. It shows the bar rejects *this* naive method *here*; it does not bound the false-accept rate in general.
- The step is a proton transfer, the case where uniform interpolation is worst. A control on a step with a looser reaction coordinate would be a weaker test of the guess method and a different test of the bar.
- It says nothing about false *rejections*: how often the bar turns away a correct mechanism is measured by negative control B, not here.
