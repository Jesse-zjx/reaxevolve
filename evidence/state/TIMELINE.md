# Campaign timeline

Generated by `scripts/reflect/build_timeline.py`. Every date here comes from a machine record: the `time` field a verdict was written with, or the `Starting time` header ORCA writes into its own output. The asset events at the end are the one exception and are marked as such.

Events are placed on two axes. **Verdicts reached** advances by work done, which makes it the better of the two: a flat stretch of the calendar axis can just as easily mean nobody was running the campaign that week. Calendar date is kept because the deposits and the asset sightings have no finer coordinate.

Neither axis is a test of whether the system improved. The campaign was not run to measure that: the easy steps went first and the frontier moved outward, exploration was human-directed and switched between three routes, and the bar itself was tightened part way through. Difficulty is therefore not held constant along either axis, so a slope that does not rise is not evidence of a system that did not improve. That comparison needs the matched benchmarks, not this file.

## Accepted edges

30 of 30 STRICT edges carry a dated verdict record, spanning 2026-06-18 to 2026-08-04. The date is when the verdict was recorded, which is the acceptance event itself and not a proxy for it.

Where a species pair was attempted more than once, the failed attempts are cached next to the accepted one, so each date is taken from a record whose status is STRICT rather than from the earliest record of any status. Dating from any record instead would backdate two of these edges by about a week. Every edge here was dated from a STRICT record.

### Against verdicts reached

180 verdicts were recorded in all, 144 of them exploratory and 36 re-runs of a pair that had already been accepted. The axis counts verdicts, not jobs launched: an attempt that died before the pipeline could judge it left no record, and the automated loop logs more dispatches than there are records in its weeks, so this is a floor on the effort spent. 13 of the 180 records carry a bare date and sit at midnight, so they order arbitrarily within their own day.

The last column is the slope, and it has to be taken per exploratory verdict: the final bins are almost entirely re-validation, and dividing by every verdict there manufactures a collapse that did not happen. It is left blank where a bin holds fewer than 5 exploratory verdicts, since a yield of 1.00 off a single verdict reads like a result. Over the run recorded so far the slope moves up and down without settling into a direction, on bins of about twenty verdicts each -- which is what an uncontrolled record of a moving frontier looks like, and is not itself a finding either way. This is a snapshot: re-running the script after further work extends the axis.

Each accepted edge also carries the number of verdicts its own species pair absorbed up to and including the accepting one (`verdicts_on_pair_to_acceptance`): median 1, range 1 to 4, and 25 of 30 passed on the pair's first verdict. So the campaign did not mostly work by retrying a pair until it cleared -- a pair that failed was usually abandoned. Any benchmark whose main metric is "guesses to STRICT" under a retry budget is therefore measuring something this campaign did not do; pass rate at a fixed budget per pair is the metric the behaviour supports.

The denominator that goes with the 30 accepted edges is here too: the verdicts cover 107 distinct species pairs, of which 67 were never accepted, and those absorbed 91 of the 180 verdicts. Reporting the accepted edges without this is reporting a numerator.

## What this cannot support

- The curve counts accepted edges. It does not separate edges produced by the automated loop from edges produced by reaction-specific methods, and the loop table above is the honest measure of the former.
- This is an observational reconstruction of a run that happened, not a controlled ablation. Nothing here isolates the effect of any single asset.
- Nothing here settles whether the system got better, in either direction. The targets were not difficulty-matched and got harder as the frontier moved out, so the slope is not a learning curve and a flat one is not a null result. Read it as the state of the record, which further work changes.
- The per-edge cost is not a difficulty-matched quantity. An easy pair attempted once and a hard pair attempted six times both appear, so the spread is mostly chemistry, and the median is not a system metric.
- 180 verdict records were indexed to date 30 edges; where an edge has several accepted records the earliest of those is used, so a date is the first time the edge cleared the bar and not the last time it was re-run.
