# Would the preregistered stopping rule have fired? — retrospective

Generated by `experiments/saturation_retrospective/analyse.py`.

**This is a retrospective analysis, not evidence that the rule stopped anything.**
The rule (`preregistration/panel.json`: K=3 windows, ε=5% relative growth) was written after the campaign it is applied to here, and the campaign was session-style rather than round-based, so no round-level metrics exist. `loop/saturation.py` enforces the rule for future runs. What follows can only say where the rule *would* have stopped.

## Inputs

- attempt stream: `evolution/attempt_vectors.jsonl`, 123 attempts, 14 distinct accepted edges
- for reference, the canonical network holds 30 accepted edges: the stream begins after some session-style work, so it is a subset and the two numbers are not expected to match
- ordering: the `time` field, the only ordering the records carry

## Constructed rounds, and why the block size is swept

The campaign has no rounds, so they have to be constructed as consecutive blocks of attempts. Block size is a free parameter, and picking one after seeing the answer would make the result meaningless — so every size below is reported and the conclusion is whatever holds across them.

| block size | blocks | rule fires at block | of total | on which condition |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 10 | 13 | 13 | 13 | zero_growth_for_K_blocks |
| 15 | 9 | never | 9 | — |
| 20 | 7 | never | 7 | — |
| 25 | 5 | never | 5 | — |
| 30 | 5 | never | 5 | — |

The rule fires at 1 of 5 block sizes (10) and not at the others, so it **is** sensitive to how rounds are constructed. Reported as such; this is a reason to treat the retrospective as weak evidence.

## ε barely differs from "no new edge at all" at this scale

ε=5% of the accepted-edge count only admits a nonzero increment once that count reaches 20. The largest count reached in this stream is 14, and at block size 20 there are 0 blocks where the ε test and a plain "d_strict == 0" test would disagree.

So at this campaign's scale the preregistered ε is close to a zero-growth rule. That is a property of the rule at small accepted-edge counts, not a flaw in the data, and it is why `loop/saturation.py` reports which of the two conditions fired instead of collapsing them.

## What this does and does not license

- It does **not** license "the loop ran until saturation". The loop did not run under this rule.
- It does not license a claim that the asset set is optimal or complete.
- It does support the narrower statement that the preregistered threshold is consistent with where this campaign's accepted-edge growth actually went, which is worth stating because a rule that would have fired on attempt three, or never, would be evidence the threshold was chosen badly.
